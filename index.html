What Is Women of MP3.com?

Women of MP3.com (WoMP3) was an intrapreneur channel on MP3.com dedicated to women in independent music — as artists, writers, producers, and listeners. Sponsored by Sephora, it launched in October 1999 and ran until 2003, communicating with its audience through a weekly newsletter, artist features, and an editorial column.

At its height, MP3.com drew millions of registered users and hundreds of thousands of daily visitors. As a channel on that platform, WoMP3 built a dedicated readership of women-in-music fans and indie artists who knew exactly who we were — even if we weren't always on the front page.

The channel's editorial voice was shared among a collective of women, gender-spectrum folx, men, and allies. Many voices, many contributors. Much of the editorial content lived in CyberMuse, a column written by editorial contributor Jianda, reviewing independent artists on the platform. CyberMuse (later titled i-Muse) ran throughout the life of the channel, spotlighting artists across gospel, jazz, pop, electronic, ambient, and more.

About This Archive

This archive is an ongoing recovery effort. What you'll find here are saved snapshots from the Wayback Machine and other sources — newsletter issues, the CyberMuse column, artist pages, and contextual pages that survived the platform's multiple ownership changes.

Many other people are working to preserve what's left of MP3.com. This collection focuses specifically on the Women of MP3.com channel and its editorial history. See the Resources page for the broader archival community's work.

Timeline

1997
MP3.com founded by Michael Robertson as an informational hub for MP3 technology and hardware.
1998–99
Platform expands into artist hosting and distribution. Becomes one of the most trafficked sites on the web. Explosive IPO in July 1999.
Oct 1999
Women of MP3.com launches, sponsored by Sephora. Weekly newsletter, artist features, and CyberMuse column begin.
2000
UMG v. MP3.com lawsuit. Court rules against MP3.com; $53.4M in damages to Universal alone. CyberMuse column continues through legal turbulence.
Aug 2001
Vivendi Universal acquires MP3.com. Platform loses independent identity under corporate ownership.
2001–03
WoMP3 continues under Vivendi. CyberMuse column runs (i-Muse branding appears in 2001 issues). Coverage gaps due to ownership transitions.
2003
CNET Networks acquires the domain. MP3.com shuts down December 2, 2003. Women of MP3.com ends with the platform.

What MP3.com Made Possible

  • ●Channels like Women of MP3.com demonstrated that niche editorial programming could build dedicated communities on the platform — well before the era of social media influencers or genre-specific streaming playlists.
  • ●It proved that consumers hungered for digital, downloadable music delivery long before Apple launched the iTunes Store.
  • ●It paved the way for democratized music distribution, showing that independent artists could bypass traditional record label gatekeepers to reach a global fanbase.
  • ●Alongside peer-to-peer services like Napster, MP3.com sat at ground zero of the legal and cultural wars that forced the multi-billion-dollar music industry to drag itself into the digital age.
