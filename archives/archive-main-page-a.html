Song Spotlight

Alicia Dara
"Business as Usual"
Lo Fi: Play


Conservatory trained Alicia Dara left Canada for New York City at 18. Honing her talents as a session singer and backup vocalist, she spent her days in music school and moonlighted in some of the city's most notorious bars. She returned to the West Coast and started her own label in 1997. Her newest CD, "Toys," features all the passionate vocals and expert guitar work that fans have come to expect.

About "Business as Usual":
"This one was inspired by a swim I took in the Malibu surf when I was a child. When my grandparents were still living, we used to spend summers with them in L.A., and the beach was always a popular trip. 'Business As Usual' is a song that's been brewing in me ever since I first stood neck-deep in the surf and felt the raw power of the waves rush over me. In a child's simple way, I realized that no matter what happened, the current of my life would always guide me toward my dreams, if I trusted and paid attention. I was born in a city by the ocean and I've come to see it as a huge metaphor for the great circle of life. It always gives me the courage to get off the proverbial couch and get moving!"

Cyber Muse by Jianda Johnson


Purple Planet
"Purple Planet"
Lo Fi: Play
Purple Planet's mix of acoustic and electronic elements breaks down nicely as you take in their highly palatable good trips. Transcending their cross between world music, drum n' bass and alternavibes like that of Garbage and Portishead, this duo creates a refreshing kind of magic, and lyrics like "You're like the first puff on my last cigarette" ("Addicted") and "Nothing could be sweeter than my auto-written destiny" ("Rearview Mirror") feed your head while you're in their dream state. In David and Cassandra's interplanetary songs of bliss, sweet, charming sentiments reach haunting, edgy heights. Old vinyl samples mix with techno licks and effect-free acoustic guitars. Meanwhile, Cassandra sings sweet, twisted melodies. Bravo for experiential tunes for the cyber-tribalist within. (My fave? "Calamity Jane" captures all these ingredients to a T.)

Video Diary - Jenn Vix



"Blind"
Download
Lo Fi (Real 28.8)
Hi Fi (Real 56+)
(2:18 minutes)



You'll need Real Player plugin to view this video.

Jenn Vix's "Blind" video was produced by Snozzberry Entertainment, (John Fitzgerald and Bill Winters). Directed by Sean Carter and Bill Winters. Shot on 35mm film and transferred to video. It received rave reviews at the Eyeball Music Video Festival in Athens, Georgia, for its style and imagery.

The video was shot over the Halloween weekend 1998, in Valhalla, New York. It was officially released on March 1, 1999, coinciding with the release of the album "Hope Springs Nocturnal."

The basic concept of the video is...the ghost of a woman comes back to warn another woman about the dangers of being involved with the stalker who took her life.

In the end, the man is captured and bound by two soul collectors. The binding scene is representative of a wiccan "binding" spell. This spell is performed to ward off and prevent a malicious person from harming you, or others.
