The Fireants
"12 Years"
Lo Fi: Play
Among WO'MP3's digital divas and sonic goddesses, we find Skie Bender--estrogenic cornerstone of The Fireants. What can we make of her? She's either all woman (road-weary poetess), or all madwoman (with Jello Biafra on the side). "12 Years," the droning, twisted tale uploaded on our illustrious web site, might give us some insight. She sings "Your fetish is the FBI/Surveillance cameras in your eyes/catch you dusting fingerprints/can't you see I'm dropping hints?" and we get it: Our nightmare is her daydream. The push-pull anti-melodies in Bender's voice waft over Kevin Jacobs' and Kelly Busby's guitar-drum "testosteronia" well. The Fireants are too loud for coffee shops, too revelatory for dive bars. Though they're not uploaded, the catchy, spaced-out Powerpop tune "Sun Go Down" and "Congratulations," a song about one woman's psychodrama, are worth shelling out the extra few bones for. Deep thinkers and performance art devotees will love this band.

What's your coping mechanism of choice? It couldn't be music, could it? Inquiring Jianda wants to know.

Kristen Banks, Pop Diva

A Connecticut native who now lives in California, Kristin Banks is catching the attention of many industry pros.

Her debut CD "House of Echoes" is a contemporary pop project, with a sound most frequently compared to Barbra Streisand and Celine Dion. Building on the critical acclaim of "House of Echoes," Kristin continues to showcase songs from the CD in live performances, as well as here on MP3.COM.

WM: Location/Occupation:
KB: San Diego/ Part-time private tutor, elementary education degree.

WM: How did you get started in the music biz?
KB: I've wanted to be a singer since 8 years old. I used to take a candle and pretend that was my microphone, and in my Grammy Award-winning pink bathrobe (the closest I had to glamour at the time!), I would strut back and forth on my "stage" and sing to all the imaginary people (couch and chairs). I found my voice at age 11, and began training at the Hartt School of Music. I attended Syracuse University as a musical theater major, and formed my first band in 1993.

WM: What advice would you give to others?
KB: Don't try to do it all by yourself! As independent free spirits, we can easily fall into that trap. Surround yourself with an enthusiastic support team-- just let it happen! I have a wonderful support team-- I just turned around and they were there. (Mom, Paul, Matt, Andy, Joe-- love you guys!)

WM: What has been the high point of your career:
KB: MP3.com, baby! In any other profession, there are outlets. Unless we are "signed" artists, we're drinking a lot of coffee! This is a way to reach people, (isn't that what we're here for?) It is yet another path to help artists lead the authentic life.

WM: What other artists do you admire?
KB: The dreamers who dream big enough to make it happen!

WM: Favorite color:
KB: Hands down, forest green!

WM: What MP3.com artists I would recommend to others?
KB: Kevin Montgomery, Patrick Murphy, Eve Selis and Faith

M2aria - M (squared) Aria

My name is Maria Nazzaro and I play rhythm guitar, banjo, flute, piano and a little alto saxophone. I fancy myself a singer/songwriter, and have written all music and lyrics for the M2aria recording (except for music on #No.7). Paul Opalach appears on this recording as my producer, and plays all instruments on most of the songs. Mark Bridgman plays bass and guitar on two songs.

I've worked as a waitress at a diner, a legal secretary, a carpenter and a housecleaner, while simultaneously trying to pursue a career in acting/music, with the occasional weekend "gig" singing in a local cover bar band. (Whew!) I am currently teaching voice at a local music school, singing in a show on the weekends and recording my next project.

I've been working at developing my craft for nine years now. I started out singing some covers in my cousin's band to make some extra cash, and to get over my extreme shyness. We played easy-listening music to a crowd that was more interested in the chicken on their plate than what was happening on stage. It was a bitch and a cure.

I've had many difficulties to overcome. I had a very tough upbringing. My mother married an African-American man back in the early '70s and we lived in a ghetto. I was often the subject of ridicule and racial slurs from my school peers and neighborhood kids because of our interracial situation and our lack of wealth. To top things off, my stepfather was very abusive. I had to overcome feeling sorry for myself and feeling like the world owed me something for all that I had to endure. I had to overcome my sister's death. I had to overcome once-upon-a-time "close friends" and lovers telling me I'll never amount to anything in the entertainment business and that I should give up my dreams. I had to overcome my lack of self-respect and insecurity.

People always ask me what's up with the "2" in M2aria... -It's symbolic to me. It's black and white, representing my parents. It represents the highs and lows I've experienced in my life. It reminds me that there are two sides to every story, and how you perceive things are not necessarily how they are. The two is also about movement, and how you will sometimes swing back and forth from two extremes until you find your "vibe." The 2 also represents love. And love, like the moon, either grows full, or will wane into a dark night.

The Sultry Eliana

Eliana is a beautifully stunning pop recording artist whose contemporary sound and vocal prowess have gained her thousands of fans all over the world. Starting from her hometown of Ponca, Nebraska, Eliana has been involved with music for 10 years. We asked Eliana to tell us a little more about her career...

WM: How did you get started in the music biz?
E: I started singing jingles for a local recording studio, and started my first professional working band at age 15.

WM: What difficulties have you faced?
E: When you are reaching for a big dream, certain realities set in such as financial set-backs in an expensive industry or maybe difficulties with an unsupportive relationship.

I have learned many lessons and have chosen to see challenges as opportunities that I have created. I welcome all challenges. It gives me an opportunity to learn and grow as a woman and as a creative artist. I have found that certain characteristics help to get through them: strength, persistence, resilience, and studying all aspects of the music industry can pave an easier road to success.

WM: What would tell other artists just starting out?
E: Surround yourself with a solid support team. Don't anyone tell you that you can't achieve your dream. I believe people can have anything they want as long as they create the opportunities and believe in themselves and work hard. Search for inner strength and don't ever, ever give up! Also don't be afraid to take risks. People who follow the crowd will never be followed by a crowd.

WM: What have been the most exciting moments of your career?
E: 1. Being chosen as an opening act for many established artists such as Sheryl Crow, Eddie Money, Trisha Yearwood, etc. at different times in my professional career.

2. Having learned my craft well, I'm able to teach others to sing and perform. Helping others develop gives me a sense of contribution and success.

3. Being featured on MP3.com allowed me to have three No. 1 hits on three different MP3.com charts simultaneously for several weeks. It also allowed me to gain exposure to 4,000 new fans due to 100,000 hits to my MP3.com Artist Page.

WM: Who are your favorite MP3.com artists?
E: Hypnogaja and Corday
Bronika Kushkuley, Child Prodigy

Bronika Kushkuley began studying piano when she was just six with the distinguished teacher Julia Bernstein of the New England Conservatory. A short year and a half later, Bronika surprised everyone by winning first prize at the International Piano competition in Washington, D.C. After that Bronika's parents started to take her piano lessons a bit more seriously, and the lessons paid off. At the age of ten, Bronika won the grand prize at the International Young Pianists Competition in Senigallia, Italy where she competed with forty-six pianists under the age of sixteen from twenty countries.

Currently twelve years old, Bronika has already established herself as a seasoned concert artist, participating in piano recitals in Massachusetts, Connecticut, New Hampshire, New York, Hawaii, Washington D.C., Germany, Austria, Holland, Italy, Switzerland and France. In addition, she has performed at the exclusive Edward Pickman Hall at the Longi School of Music in 1998. This summer Bronika performed at the International Music Festival de Colmar, France and was a student-in-residence at Verbier Festival, Switzerland.

Video Maven - The Groove Kings

The Groove Kings came out of nowhere-- without the aid of a record company-- to put a song on the International pop charts for six months. Here are they in the video "Everybody Knows."


Streaming Video: Watch Now! (2:56 min)

"The video for Everybody Knows was filmed in the Montreal plateau area in time for the release of our 1st album.

The filming took place in a restaurant after it closed starting at about midnight. The windows were blocked with theatrical curtains from the outside so when the sun came up the next morning it would still be dark inside.

We invited all our friends to play the part of the audience. We did hire some actors for the story within the video. It was the first video we ever made and I was excited. It was however, a very long night. The filming lasted about 20 hours.

To me, the video worked well with the song. I find the way the band was observing the events of the video was how I interpreted the song. Like someone outside the scene. I remember trying to lip-synch the song but I couldn't, so I just sang along with the music.

In the last scene as the band walks out into the street, you can see the street lights shut off. This was just a lucky accident. It was about 5 in the morning."

-Irene Marc of The Groove Kings
