Cyber Muse Weekly by Jianda Johnson


Umbrella
"Bored"
Lo Fi: Play
The confident strength of Naomi Kukoff's bluesy/jazzy voice alluringly guides you into Umbrella's world. Automatically, you feel cozy there. Umbrella's still a new ensemble though, and it shows in a couple of yin-yang areas. Musically, they're strong songwriters and you get the feeling they love to jam with each other (yang, all the way). The one yin side of the band has to do with momentum. "Bored" for instance, with its strolling Rickie Lee Jones-ish arrangement, breaks into a panoramic (albeit briefly so) chorus that really drives home the point of the song--it works best here. However, each song's sonic dynamics often creep from a peaceful Sunday-afternoon vibe to a big-production Saturday-night-gig vibe, within measures and without a buildup. These seasoned musicians' diversity of influences mesh well together, which is a rare find. It just seems like they're still riding the waves, right on the crest of an ideal balance.

Video Maven - Fisher

This week's Video Maven is the ultra talented Fisher, who has found incredible success through the Internet. In fact, she has had nearly 800,000 downloads combined from numerous music sites on the Internet! Here is her video for "Any Way." Enjoy!

Streaming Video: Watch Now! (4:08 min)
Hosted by 52nd Stream Media


Fisher originally had no intention of shooting a video for "Any Way," as they didn't have any phone calls to return from MTV or VH-1, and nobody seemed in the mood to throw money in their laps. Then the phone rang. It was longtime friend and budding director/producer Doug Green. "I need a new video for my reel," said Green. "If I organize everything, pay for everything, write the script and shoot it, can I do a video for your song, 'Any Way'?" It was a difficult decision for Fisher to make, but three seconds later, they replied with a resounding, "Yes!"

A three-day shoot, the main setting was at a quaint country church in Fillmore, California, nestled among acres of orange trees. The honorable Rev. Lee was most gracious, and forgave all us L.A. folks for our potty mouths, choosing rather to see the good in us.

Green's script revolved around a woman (Kathy Fisher) who couldn't commit to a relationship with her lover (Jonathon Stewart), but didn't want to let go of him, either, thus haunting him on the day of his wedding to someone new. Only he can see her spirit, weaving down the isle behind his lovely bride (Eris Huemer), whispering in his ear as he takes his vows and, finally, flying over his head as he prepares to leave the church to start his new life. "We had originally wanted to do an effect that would make it really look like I was flying over Stu's head", said Fisher, "but we didn't have an extra $70,000! Thus, at the end of the video, it looks like I committed suicide. Really...I wouldn't go that far over a guy! Anyway, it was the best $25,000 we never spent, and we are thrilled to have something so wonderful available on the web!"
