Station Pages Are Here! back to top...

What is a Station page, you ask? A Station is your own personal forum on MP3.com. Any user on the site can create one to showcase music they find on the site and would like to share. Any song that is freely available by any artist on MP3.com can be put on your very own page. You can create a Station to review music, share music with your friends, speak your mind or virtually anything else. It's your page! How do you begin?

1. Go to My Mp3 (you can find it in the navigation bar at the top of the page.)
2. Log In.
3. Click on My Pages (top navigation bar...)
4. Click on Create new Station (left navigation area)
5. Create your unique page.

Go to the Station listing to see what others are creating. If you have a page that is relevant to the Women of MP3.com, send us the URL. We will add a link to your site!

Kym Tuvim: Writer, Musician, Person... back to top...

"Music has always been an integral part of my life, with musicians on both sides of my family, I started playing piano when I was 4 or 5. Before I was 10, I was writing my own music - first on piano and then on guitar - always my way of digging through life and sorting things out.

In 1998, I released a self-titled cd and quickly became immersed in the business side of the independent music industry, learning the ropes of distribution and promotion and getting my record out to as many djs and reviewers as I could. My work paid off - the record fared well, receiving airplay all over the country (on mostly independent stations), favorable reviews and in 1998, it was picked as a Top 12 Independent Release by the Performing Songwriter Magazine. As a performer, I love playing 'listening rooms' and have toured both the West Coast and New England.

That said, one of the most difficult and surprising things that has come about since becoming involved in the 'music industry' is the discovery that I need to actively create balance between being in business with being an artist. I'm still getting a handle on it! As time passes, the learning curve still goes up and down, but I'm writing more now. And, I would say that my highest goal is to continue to grow as a writer, musician and person. As long as I'm creating music and saying something meaningful, I'll be happy."

Tales of a Siren - Siryn of Temple of Rain back to top...

si'ren n. 1, A mythical alluring sea nymph. 2, An alluring and dangerous woman. 3, A device for producing sound.

"As a singer, seer, Co-Founder of the rock band Temple of Rain, belly dancer, high priestess and founder of an ancient mystery school for women, my voice has been the tool of the Goddess for thousands of years. The voice is a powerful link to our ancient selves, as the whales in their sonar waves, we too have secrets encoded with our higher purposes only to be found in utilizing our own unique voices. It is time again for sounds that heal and enlighten the planet.

As an ordained teacher of a fully enlightened being and of a lineage of Mahayogis and high sorcery, I utilize this tool in various ways. For we know that the Goddess has many sides, as oceans can heal, seduce, give life, or destroy, I use my voice, and my whole body as an instrument to do the same, embodying the force and channelling what's needed at the time. Todays "Women Of Rock" (the sirens DO sing among the rocks) has had a spot for me since I can remember.

In this life, I've been brought from the deep waters into a family of musicians which has allowed me to continue my work. I'd sneak a nap in a bassdrum back stage at my father's concerts, (I've always found music soothing no matter how loud). During his tours my Dad and sometimes the king himself, (yes Elvis Presley) would call me from Las Vegas. When the King gave me a call for the last time he told me to keep singing, and sent me a rose with a little card. It was sort of an initiation into the world of Rock n' Roll to carry on the flash, glitz, and mystery of what it is all about. Shortly after, he died. I was quite young, yet somehow I knew my fate was sealed...."

Artist Spotlight on Sonia Rubinsky... sponsored by Oxygen back to top...

"I love playing the piano, and it seems that this love was there from a very early age: I begged my mother to bring me to a teacher at age 5. She thought I was too young. I remember going to the piano at that age and making sounds, and putting my ears near the keyboard...

I started half a year later, and by 6 I gave my first recital! By twelve I played with orchestra (Mozart Concerto - the Coronation - pretty hard piece!). I had already won several competitions and I was very proud to be earning my first fee as a real professional at age twelve. It seems to me now a pretty outstanding beginning, but I do not remember considering myself a child prodigy, actually, on the contrary, I received a very hard training from my first piano teacher in Brazil: It was never good enough.

I later studied in Israel, Paris, Canada, and NY where I went to the Juilliard School for a Masters and Doctorate. My training was very rigorous, and I always knew I wanted to be a concert pianist. However it is an extremely difficult career choice. It is underpaid - considering that to train a full fledged musician you basically need about 25 years - much more than most professions! I also encounter a lot of difficulty being a woman. I hope I am not paranoid - but I think managers and presenters do not take you very seriously - not to talk about other fellow (men) musicians - I had a few bad experiences with conductors... Over time resentment builds up - I work so hard and than to be treated as a pretty piano player - is so infuriating!!!

With all the difficulties in building, developing and maintaining a career what drives me still is the love for the instrument, the love for music, and the striving for a dream of beauty and perfection. I think deep down all artists are dreamers. I am one for sure."
