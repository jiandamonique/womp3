Bria Fuentes, Not Just Another Pretty Face. back to top...

"It has been a very long road for me in this music business! I am a songwriter as well as a singer so, the only problem was lack of money and connections to get my music out there! I have worked very hard to have a demo CD and web site!(thanks to MP3.com) Everyone hears my music and always asks, 'Who did your songs?' When I tell them that I wrote it I feel second guessed. There always has to be a man involved for it to be believable! You can do it all on your own if you have the desire and discipline!

I want people to respect me for my talent and not think that I made it because I am attractive! I swear if you look good people always think that you slept your way to the top. It is SO unfair! I don't let it bother me because I believe in myself and I know that God will guide me in the right direction. I hope all you lady artists out there won't ever let anyone tell you that you can't do it! Do whats in your heart and your dreams will come true!" -Bria Fuentes

Good Times are Here for Yvonne Smith back to top...

"I don't think there has ever been a better time to be a woman in this industry. Just this year, the Dixie Chicks and Faith Hill have been at the top of the Billboard charts, which is an enormous feat! The women are doing better than many of the men right now, and I just hope the trend continues long enough for me to get a piece of the action!

For a long time, the big heads of the industry in Nashville had believed that since women were the major purchasers of country music, they wanted to buy music made by men. If you were a woman artist, you were supposed to be a girl-next-door type, showing minimal cleavage and certainly no navel! Acts like diamond-selling Shania Twain have helped prove that the public wants fresh, modern, and yes- even sexy, entertainers. So the industry is changing. Careers aren't lasting as long as they used to, but if you can manage to carve out a niche and leave your mark, you've accomplished something. I think now more than ever the public and the music industry have embraced women in music, and I am ready to be a part of it all!" -Yvonne Smith

Yvonne's Picks: Jewel Thieves * The Assholes

Sandra Booker, Jazz Diva! back to top...

"I think women in today's music industry are taking on the roles of arranger, composer, engineer, etc., in addition to being performers (specifically vocalist) and it is going to have an important impact on the music. In the genre of jazz, the presence of women is gaining significant momentum and offers a wide variety of opportunities that seemed off limits before. This ranges from colleagues like Patrice Rushen and Brenda Russell music conducting prime television events to Maria Schneider conducting a big band and orchestrating to women jazz instrumentalists like Regina Carter, Terri Lyne Carrington, and Geri Allen establishing a powerful presence as band leaders; not solely in the role of vocalist.

Many women are producing the music we are hearing in jazz (and other styles of music) and though it's on a smaller scale, the work being done is paramount. I am at a place in my career when women's individuality and pioneering spirit are the new approach to jazz music and are bound to result in a major musical eruption. This is a truly a time for collaboration and celebration." -Sandra Booker

Who does Sandra admire? "Scarlet's Web is a remarkable songwriter. The style of music is alternative but I think this woman is going to make major strides in the music industry. I recently caught a performance of hers at LA's Genghis Cohen and it was fantastic. This is someone I would strongly recommend giving a listen to and promoting."

Artist Spotlight on Mishiko Fronczak of DIM... Sponsored by Oxygen back to top...

"I have found that being a woman in the music industry has been a true test of my belief and confidence in my lyrics and music. Even though boundaries are being broken as they have never been before, there is still the challenge to be heard and taken seriously as a woman. I guess that challenge is what keeps me going. Understanding common things like recording, or copyright laws, to booking gigs has given me some insight and perspective to being an integral part of the industry.

I've been fortunate to have four great guys in the band who contribute to our sound and ideas. It's been difficult to explain to people what we sound like, seeing that our sound is a melting pot of ideas. I think it's pretty cool that we get so many varied responses to what our music relates to, because I feel that the music we're doing is making a mark by having a different flavor. Some Goth, some alternative, rock, jazz and classical influences all contribute to our sound.

As the lead singer for DIM, I feel that my greatest challenge is being a woman. I wanted to prove to everybody that just because I am a woman doesn't mean that my edge has to be a softer, kinder, more 'feminine' approach. I figured, why can't a woman be comfortable with her interaction with the audience without having to show her tits off or be a size 5? My confidence doesn't stem from those things, only my voice does. And by being able to accomplish those feats and getting recognition for my musical and lyrical talents is what makes money -less gigs and late nights after my other job (the one that pays the bills) worth the while." -Mishiko Fronczak
