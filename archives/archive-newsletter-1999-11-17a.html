Can't seem to find what you're searching for?

So, how do you even begin to find good music your interested in on MP3.com? Besides checking out the Women of MP3.com of course, there are many ways to find the tunes your craving. We all know we can find songs by genre, songs by region, artist by genre, artist by region, alphabetical listing, weekly top 40, weekly bottom 40, and search. But here are some lessor known tips:

1. Type in the name of your favorite popular artists in the search box to find music that has a similar sound. For example, type in "Mazzy Star" in the search engine, and 41 artists with the similar surreal sound come up.

2. Pay attention to the Featured Artist Spots. These are carefully selected slots of artists we think are awesome. In addition, look at your favorite genre's "e-zines". They are located on the under the "Music Scene" section on the top genre pages. If your favorite genre does not have an e-zine, stay tuned, it probably will soon.

3. Check out the Payola Songs sections on the Main Music page, and every top genre page. This is the artist "paid promotion" area of the page. These artists really want you to hear their music, and I've personally found some real gems. Check it out!

Have any other search tip? We want to know!

Eve Selis shares her thoughts on being a female artist today:

"I've been singing for most of my life and this is by far the most exciting time to be a female artist. Alanis, Sarah McLachlan, Shawn Colvin, Lucinda Williams and many others opened so many doors for us in the mid-90s. In the music industry today, in this post-Lilith Fair environment, women are respected far more for their talent and writing than at any time in recent history. I feel a shift has occured that will outlast any industry trend, and that is that women artists are validated by their art and not by the length of their skirt or how much cleavage they're willing to show. Prejudice and sexism remain for sure, and in many cases we're our own worst enemies when it comes to buckling under the pressure, but it has certainly gotten much better."

"MP3.com has been a very nurturing environment for female artists. Look at the charts. Women rock this place. I was privileged to be a part of the first-ever Club.MP3.com showcase in San Diego and the bill was chock full of talented women who I believe truly support each other and who feel a kinship with the other women on MP3.com. We are a vital part of not one, but two of the most phenomenal movements to rock the status quo, male-dominated music industry ever: The shift in gender respect and the digital/Internet revolution. I've never been more excited about my career and the future of music in general."

Thanks, eve

Artist Spotlight on Nadine Shanti... sponsored by Oxygen

Ms. Shanti gives us some insight into her career and how she contributes to society...

"I started writing music in 1997 because I wanted to put more positive, uplifting music out into our society. I also didn't like the image of women that I was seeing portrayed in the pop culture. I am up for an attractive image but I want something that is more relaxed: not always so plastered over from head to foot in sex. Hence the title "Down Home" and the image in jeans and a hat."

"I feel a responsibility to give back to our society as much as I have been given. Therefore I collect for one of Seattle's charities (Childhaven) whenever I perform live. Childhaven is an organization that intercedes in child abuse situations. The children are taken into special classes where they learn different behaviors for dealing with anger and frustration and the parents are counseled as well."
