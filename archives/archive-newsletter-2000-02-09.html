Melinda Klump
"The Short Story"
Lo Fi: Play
Melancholy, brooding, droning...these words waft up while you're digging Melinda Klump'svoice, chanting lightly over lyrics like "we walk down the street/two blocks seem like twenty (from All Over Again)." Klump cites Jeff Buckley as one of her influences--not surprising. The dark cello, too, moans like a drowning maiden, adding more dissonant color to her vignettes.

Portishead, while 180 degrees opposite of Klump, elicits similar emotions: You want to save the character, the anti-heroine brought to life, but you know the she has to--and doesn't want to--save herself. She's married to sadness. Even as she sings "hello laughter/hello light filling the sky (from 'Goodbye')," you're not completely convinced.

In her imaginary landscapes, maybe once there was a Shangri-La, but it seems she's lost her way back. She's mourning. She's asking for your help, or maybe she's just asking you to listen. Klump consumes, captivates and puts words to your moodiness.

Video Maven - Barbara Lewis

Singer/songwriter Barbara Lewis is a unique artist who combines beautiful melodies and a haunting, wide-range voice with lyrics that carry a strong social message. Her music expresses time-honored yearnings--searching for freedom, embracing life's mysteries and staying true to your heart's desires. A storyteller for modern times, Lewis incorporates song and spoken text in her unique performances. Here are two video clips from "Hara's Quest," by singer/songwriter Barbara Lewis:

"BODY" tells the story of a woman named Hara, who visits a futuristic spa in Indonesia, hoping that a unique body massage will unlock some troubling memories. The masseur rubs her with soft electric cloths that will increase her ability to remember her past. The room is filled with an intoxicating aroma. Attendants whisper in her ear to assure her that the answers to her questions lie within. They say; "Go deeper. Hear the voices? Are they calling out? Listen! Remember!"

Body (1.05 min)

"LULLABY" is a serene song about letting go of the past and surrendering to the present. Lewis dedicated the song to her brother, Jim, after his death. Both videos were filmed in Montreal, Canada, and produced by writer/director Nicholas Regush.

These songs are found on the CD "Hara's Quest," a fantasy adventure of a woman who unlocks her natural visionary powers. The year is 2023. The story tells of Hara's search for a mysterious island, which she believes is her true home. On her journey, she encounters spirits, demons, magical dolphins and extraterrestrials. She relives humanity's ancient past and envisions its startling future.

Congrats to the Totally Launch your Career contest winner ARIKA KIMBLE!!!

TLC takes the FANMAIL concept one step further by choosing one of their most ardent and talented fans to open their show in Atlanta on January 29, 2000. Arika Kimble was chosen from thousands of MP3.com artists to open the TLC show and share the stage with her idols. It truly was a once-in-a-lifetime experience for Arika, which she describes in the following Q&A.;


Arika backstage with friends


Meet Arika Kimble, whose sheer originality, composition and star quality made her grand prize winner of the MP3.com Totally Launch Your Career contest. As the winner, Kimble recently got the opportunity to open for TLC at a concert in the trio's hometown of Atlanta.

Given Kimble was in competition with more than 2,500 artists, it's no surprise she serves up some of the hottest songs on our site. Her D.A.M. CD, "My Kind Of Guy," is a collection of mesmerizing grooves big on gentle beats and Kimble's crisp vocals.

We recently spoke with Kimble from her home in Michigan to talk about her career and performing with TLC.

So when did your singing career start?
Early '96. I actually started out with a group that didn't last very long. I ended up branching out with a solo career.

Did you want to do your own thing?
Definitely. I did mostly background and not much lead. So once the group was like "we're gonna do this and we're gonna do that," I was asking myself, do I really want to be here?

Was taking the solo route scary at first?
Heck yeah! I'm shy. If you knew me (when I was in a group), you would say that I've grown a lot since then. Then I was really, really shy. It took a lot of for me to get up on stage and sing something that I recorded on my own and in front of everyone.

Talk about being baptized by fire? Did that old shyness resurface when you performed in front of thousands at the TLC show in Atlanta?
Heck yeah! But it wasn't as severe as I thought I would be. Even with the little shows that I did, I always got the jitters. But the more I performed, the more I got into it and the less my stage fright came back at me.

How do you think you did after the show was over?
I think it went very well. And I was very excited. Every minute of it was like, "Oh my God! Is this really happening to me?"

Are you a TLC fan?
Oh yes, definitely? DEF-I-NIT-LEEE. I got a picture with T-Boz right before they went on. Just a brief little second, but that was enough for me. I got a handshake, too! And a "Oh, hello. How are you?"

I got to meet them and I got the opportunity to do something I love to do on a grand scale. I was so excited start to finish. There wasn't one moment I wasn't excited, except when I got a headache from not eating. But that passed.
--By Rosanna Kandel
