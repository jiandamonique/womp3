Cyber Muse Weekly by Jianda Johnson


Xandra
"Leave Him
To Me"
Lo Fi: Play
Xandra sparks memories of 1970s and 80s Soft Pop, best exemplified on her Stacy Lattisaw cover, "Let Me Be Your Angel." Compilations like "Spirit of 73" validate that women's music was just as heartfelt then as it is now. Music makers like Carole King, Ambrosia (that's right, men too!) and Olivia Newton John exuded lush, unabashedly sweet fireside pop vocal stylings. Often more important than the lyrics themselves, soft pop and rock then was all about the production. Atmosphere. Here--without being hackneyed or trite--Xandra's layered harmonies are transcendent, radio-friendly and warm. Today's pop milieu pretends too often to be "cool" and trendy, while this artist adds a modern spin, even to these songs that reminisce. I so enjoy (and miss) this genuine sound. You GO, Xandra.

If none of those artists' names ring a bell, please don't email Jianda... Her age is already showing.

Video Maven - ISHI

It all began on a San Francisco cable car in the summer of '99...two touristy web-conference attendees introduce themselves by the same first name and so begins a lifelong (well, seven months, at least) friendship.

ISHI front woman Lea Pritchard is currently working with director Lee Lance on an enhanced CD for the band's debut album--due out in just a few months. Lee was in the Charlotte, North Carolina area in October of '99 and Lea was able to book a spur-of-the-moment gig at Living Art, a small performance and gallery space. "This is a great place where people have really listened to our music, and although the whole thing took place on such short notice, the owners were totally supportive and gave us a couple of hours to do an impromptu, filmable show." The original plan was to just get some good footage to use in the enhanced CD--the music video was actually an afterthought.

After reviewing the footage of "Caf� America" (shot on a Sony Digital8 camera), the band decided it would be great to have a video of this song to give audiences a better understanding of who they really are. The live performance footage and the other supporting visuals were edited together with the studio-recorded version of "Caf�"--on a total budget of two Digital8 tapes.
