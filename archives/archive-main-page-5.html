Amanda Emery
"Amanda Emery"
Lo Fi: Play
Emergy. Emergy. As I listened to this album-length body of work over and over, that non-word kept asserting itself: EMERGY. Maybe it's because of this collection's energy: Amanda Emery, powered by influences like Kenny Loggins, comes off cool, mixing pop and jazz (see: Loggins' "This is It")--the "quiet storm" sound. Emergy? Could have to do with "merging." Amanda's hubby Jim backs her up with an impressive array of instrumentation, and the musical progressions are constantly moving, playing with melody and rhythm without being too flashy. The fact that they're in a relationship, merged, adds a nice color to the romantic flavor of the lyrics and the passion in Emery's voice. "Emergy," too, might mean, "emerges." "Wild Ride" and "It Takes You Back" are the standout cuts here. Amanda and Jim, your "emergy" must makes fellow Canadian indie music peers mighty proud.

Jianda will show you her Kenny Loggins if you'll show her yours.

Video Maven - NAVIV

NAVIV is definitely telling her own story of grooves in a way only she can. The singer/songwriter/guitarist lets her music live in the clubs of New York City by night. By day, she works out her eclectic approach to writing, recording and letting people know about her sound. Regulars on the NYC music scene regard her as The High Priestess of Funky Folk." The following is the story behind the her first video "Every Face."

The Basics
The "Every Face" video was shot largely in New York City. Most of the shooting took place at DeWitt Clinton Park, a public park in the Hell's Kitchen area of Manhattan.

Production
The "Every Face" video was shot with a crew of volunteers and friends during two hot days in July. We shot it on mini DV tape with a minimal amount of extra lighting and on a shoestring budget. Post production was done on a cranky old AVID.

The Meaning behind the Song
"Every Face" attempts to show that each individual that we encounter during the course of a day has a life that should be valued. The song takes a look at the isolation that one can feel in a place like New York.

Stories from the Shoot
There were a few surprises that we did not encounter during the planning of the shoot. We did not have a permit to film, so we were almost shut down by the park officials. We also had the great fortune of having a little league of about 200 ballplayers unexpectedly join us for part of the video. Somewhere along the way, a nationally broadcast cable access show called "Changing America" picked up our video for its viewers. Overall, producing the video was a great way for us to gain wider exposure and get our feet wet in other areas.

Craig, the director of the video and NAVIV would like to thank the crew--Noel, Judith, David, Tarshay, Bones and "the Mormons" for their help.
